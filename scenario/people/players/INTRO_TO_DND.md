# Intro to D&D — Everything You Need Before Session One

Read this once, and your character's own PRIMER file will make a lot more sense.

## What Even Is This?

D&D is a story you build together at the table. Jeff is the DM (Dungeon Master) — he describes the world, plays every character who isn't yours, and adjudicates what happens. You describe what *your* character tries to do. Dice decide the outcome whenever it's risky or uncertain.

## The Golden Rule

Your character sheet is not a list of the only things you're allowed to do. It's a list of things your character is *especially* good at. You can always attempt anything a person could reasonably attempt — climb a wall, bluff a guard, throw a chair, jump a gap. Just say what you want to try, in plain language, and Jeff will tell you what (if anything) to roll.

## The Dice, Quickly

- **d20** (20-sided die) is the one you'll roll constantly — it decides whether things succeed.
- **d4, d6, d8, d10, d12** are used for damage and healing amounts.
- Notation like "1d8 + 3" means: roll one 8-sided die, add 3 to the result.

## The Core Loop

1. Jeff describes a situation.
2. You say what your character does.
3. If it's uncertain or risky, you roll a d20 and add a relevant modifier.
4. That total gets compared to a target number.
5. Jeff narrates what happens.

That's the whole game, repeated endlessly in different contexts.

## The Six Ability Scores

Every character has six stats, each with a **score** (roughly 8–18 for a starting character) and a **modifier** (a smaller number, usually −1 to +4, that's what actually gets added to your rolls — it's already calculated on your sheet, you never do the math from the raw score).

- **Strength** — lifting, breaking, hitting hard in melee
- **Dexterity** — reflexes, sneaking, ranged attacks, dodging
- **Constitution** — toughness, stamina, resisting poison/exhaustion
- **Intelligence** — reasoning, memory, arcane knowledge
- **Wisdom** — perception, intuition, willpower
- **Charisma** — force of personality, persuasion, performance

## The Three Kinds of d20 Rolls

- **Ability Check** — "can I do this?" (climb, persuade, notice something). Roll d20 + modifier vs. a target number Jeff sets called a **DC** (Difficulty Class).
- **Attack Roll** — d20 + your attack bonus vs. the target's **AC** (Armor Class — how hard they are to hit). Meet or beat it, you hit.
- **Saving Throw** — d20 + modifier vs. someone else's **DC** (usually a spellcaster's). This is you resisting something being done *to* you.

Higher is always better. Meeting or beating the number is a success.

## Advantage and Disadvantage

Sometimes circumstances make something easier or harder. **Advantage** means roll two d20s and take the higher result. **Disadvantage** means roll two and take the lower. Jeff will tell you when either applies — you don't need to track this yourself.

## Your Turn in Combat

Combat runs in rounds; everyone acts once per round, in an order rolled at the start (initiative). On your turn you get:
- **A Move** — walk some distance (usually 30 ft, character sheet says exactly how far).
- **An Action** — the main thing you do: attack, cast a spell, dash, hide, help an ally, and a few others.
- **A Bonus Action** — a smaller extra thing, but *only* if something specific grants you one. Most characters don't have one every turn — check your PRIMER.
- **A Reaction** — one per round, usable any time (even outside your own turn) if something specific lets you.

You can split your move before and after your action.

## Hit Points and Getting Hurt

HP is how much punishment your character can take before going down. Damage subtracts from it; healing adds back. At 0 HP you fall unconscious and start rolling **death saves** at the start of each of your turns: roll a d20 unmodified — 10 or higher is a success, under 10 is a failure. Three successes and you stabilize; three failures and you die. A natural 20 brings you back to 1 HP, awake. Nobody's expected to die easily on a normal encounter — Jeff will make real danger clear before it happens.

## Resting

- **Short rest** (~1 hour in-game): some abilities recharge; you can spend a Hit Die to heal a bit.
- **Long rest** (~8 hours, usually overnight): full heal, most abilities and spell slots come back.

Your PRIMER lists exactly what refills on which kind of rest for your character.

## Spellcasting, Briefly

Some characters cast spells instead of (or alongside) attacking. Two kinds:
- **Attack-roll spells** — you roll to hit, same as a weapon.
- **Save spells** — the target rolls against *your* spell save DC instead.

**Cantrips** are spells you can cast unlimited times. Leveled spells cost a **spell slot**, a limited resource that refills on a rest. Your PRIMER already tells you which of your spells work which way — you don't need to memorize the distinction, just glance at the note when you cast something new.

## Talking in Character (or Not)

Totally optional. "I ask the guard about the noise" works exactly as well as doing a voice. Do whatever's comfortable — nobody's grading performance.

## Quick Glossary

- **AC (Armor Class)** — how hard a creature is to hit with an attack.
- **DC (Difficulty Class)** — the target number for a check or save.
- **HP (Hit Points)** — how much damage a creature can take.
- **Modifier** — the small bonus/penalty added to a roll, derived from an ability score.
- **Proficiency Bonus** — a flat bonus added to rolls you're trained in; grows slowly as you level up.
- **Concentration** — some spells require ongoing focus; you can only concentrate on one at a time, and taking damage can break it.
- **Critical Hit** — rolling a natural 20 on an attack roll always hits and deals extra damage. A natural 1 always misses, regardless of modifiers.
