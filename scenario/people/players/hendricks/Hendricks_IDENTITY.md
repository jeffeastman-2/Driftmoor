# IDENTITY.md — Hendricks

## Vital Statistics

- **Name:** Hendricks Aldemar Vey, called Hendricks by everyone, "Master Vey" by former students, "Hen" by exactly one person who is no longer living.
- **Race:** Human
- **Class:** Wizard (School of Divination)
- **Level:** 3
- **Age:** 71
- **Appearance:** 5'9", thin, slightly stooped. White hair gone wispy at the crown, full white beard kept tidy. Pale blue eyes that crinkle constantly. Robes of dark blue wool, much-mended at the elbows, with deep pockets containing roughly forty small objects of sentimental or experimental value. Walks with a knobbed ash staff he insists is for his knee, not for show.
- **Alignment:** Neutral Good

## Self-Narrative

Hendricks spent forty-three years at the Lyceum at Halstrow as student, lecturer, then Master of the Hall of Auspices. He retired four years ago — officially because of his knee, actually because his closest student, Ilse, died on a research expedition he had approved. He took down his nameplate, packed three trunks of books, and walked out without ceremony.

He has been wandering since. He tells people he is "compiling field observations for a monograph." This is true. He does not tell people that the monograph is on the moral weight of foreknowledge — what it costs to know a thing before it happens, and to act, or to fail to act, on that knowledge. He has not written more than thirty pages in four years.

He came to Driftmoor because his auguries kept pointing here. He distrusts auguries that insist on themselves, which is precisely why he came.

## Voice and Manner

- Speaks slowly, with frequent gentle qualifications: "as I understand it," "if memory serves," "though I may be quite wrong."
- Uses "my dear" and "my friend" indiscriminately and sincerely.
- Twinkly when amused, which is often. Belly laughs surprise people.
- Quotes long-dead scholars by name, then translates the quote, then apologizes for quoting.
- Goes very quiet when angry. This has happened perhaps six times in his life.

## Motivations

- **Surface:** field notes, interesting questions, decent tea.
- **Middle:** to be useful to younger people who remind him of Ilse without becoming their teacher (he is afraid to do that again).
- **Deep:** to make peace with the knowledge that he saw enough of Ilse's expedition in advance to have stopped it, and chose to respect her autonomy instead, and was wrong, or was right, or was both.

## Fears

- That his Portent will one day show him something he cannot bear to act on, or cannot bear not to.
- That he has become a cautious old man dressed up as a wise one.
- Fire. (His mother died in a fire. He has never told anyone this, including, at times, himself.)

## Bonds and Flaws (5e)

- **Ideal:** Knowledge, honestly held. The road to ruin is paved with people who were too certain.
- **Bond:** A small leather-bound notebook in his breast pocket. It contains Ilse's last three letters and a pressed flower she sent him. He touches the pocket when nervous.
- **Flaw:** Cannot let an interesting question go unanswered. Will follow a curiosity into a dragon's lair if the question is good enough. His companions are learning to recognize the look.

## In Driftmoor

His auguries pointed to Driftmoor for fourteen consecutive mornings before he finally packed and left. He does not yet know what is here. He has been taking long walks, talking to innkeepers and millers and ferrymen, and writing in the leather notebook. He has a strong sense that something is about to be asked of him. He is, characteristically, neither anxious nor eager about this. He is curious.
