## Chamber Descriptions

### Entry Chamber
The crack in the cliff face opens into a low, wide chamber, no taller than a
tall man at its highest point. Daylight bleeds in through the entrance as a
pale wedge that dies quickly against the dark stone. The floor is uneven —
old rockfall, water-smoothed in places. The air is colder than it should be
for the season, and unnaturally still; the wind that scoured the Reach above
does not follow you down here. Listening, you become aware of a faint, distant
sound from deeper in: not wind, not water — something in between, almost
rhythmic.

### First Passage
A short downward passage of damp, dark stone, sloping noticeably underfoot.
The walls narrow enough that two cannot easily walk abreast. The faint sounds
from deeper in grow more distinct here without ever quite resolving. Footsteps
carry strangely — sometimes ahead of you, sometimes behind.

### Echo Chamber
The largest space in the caverns. The ceiling rises beyond the reach of
torchlight into a vault of shadow. Every footstep, every breath, every word
returns from the walls a beat too late and slightly altered, as if the chamber
were repeating it back wrong on purpose. In the center of the floor, partly
buried, lies a slab of the same dark stone as the obelisk above. It hums
faintly when approached. Carvings ring the walls — the same script that marks
the cliff face and the obelisk's base, but more of it, denser, as though this
is where the message actually begins.

### Rocky Passage
A narrow squeeze westward off the Echo Chamber, choked on one side by a fall
of loose rock. Passage requires turning sideways in places. The stone here
feels older — not the smooth, water-worn cave of the entrance but something
hewn, though by what tools and to what end is no longer clear.

### Deep Passage
A steep descent. The chill deepens. Sounds from the chambers above are
swallowed entirely; what remains is a quieter, closer kind of silence — the
silence of a place that has not been listened to in a very long time. The
walls here bear no carvings.

### Bone Chamber
A roughly circular chamber, low-ceilinged, dry. Skeletal remains lie against
the far wall, the bones grey-yellow with decades of cave dust. Three skulls.
The positions tell a story you would rather not finish reading: one set of
remains clutches a holy symbol against its breastbone; another lies sprawled
with one arm outflung, as if reaching for something or someone; the third is
curled in a corner, alone. Whatever happened here happened fast and ended
badly.

### Pool Passage
A steep descent. The chill deepens. Sounds from the chambers above are
swallowed entirely; what remains is a quieter, closer kind of silence — the
silence of a place that has not been listened to in a very long time. The
walls here bear no carvings.

### Pool Cavern
A long, low cavern with a still, black pool occupying most of the floor. The
water reflects nothing — no torchlight, no faces — though the surface is
absolutely undisturbed. The far wall shows the dark scar of a collapsed
tunnel, choked with rubble. The pool is cold even by the caverns' standards.
Standing near it, the obelisk's hum from the chambers above can be felt
faintly through the stone.

## Items

### torch_bracket
An iron bracket fixed to the wall just inside the entrance. Rust-pitted and
ancient. Whatever torch it once held is long gone. The bracket is too corroded
to reuse. *(Not pickup-able; flavor.)*

### wall_scratches
Tally marks scratched into the rock near the entrance with the point of
something hard — a knife, perhaps, or a piece of obelisk shard. The strokes
are weathered and uneven, but the count is legible: nineteen vertical marks,
crossed by a slanting twentieth that trails off as though the hand making it
faltered. *(Not pickup-able; flavor.)*

### carved_symbols
The walls of the Echo Chamber are densely incised with the same angular
script that marks the cliff face and the obelisk's base — but here the
carving runs in unbroken lines, paragraph after paragraph, like a tablet
rather than a sign. A scholar of dead languages might recognize that this is
where the message actually *says* something. Aeliana cannot read it; neither
can anyone else currently in Driftmoor. Elyssa would pay handsomely for a
careful rubbing. *(Not pickup-able; rubbings can be made with parchment and
charcoal — 30 minutes per panel.)*

### obelisk_fragment
A slab of the obelisk's dark stone, half-buried in the chamber floor. About
the size of a tavern table on its side. It is warm to the touch in a place
that has no warmth, and it hums in the chest the way the obelisk above does,
though quieter. The hum responds — strengthens — when the obelisk is
mentioned aloud, or when blood touches the stone. The slab cannot be moved by
ordinary means. This is the source of the caverns' wrongness; while it
remains here, the shadowbeasts will keep coming. *(Not pickup-able as-is;
significant ritual or magic required to contain or remove.)*

### companion_remains
Three skeletons, their gear long since rotted to scraps of leather and
verdigris buckles. Father Elden Greybeard would know them at a glance — Sera,
Marrick, and Old Tomas. Restoring them to consecrated ground in Driftmoor
matters to Elden more than any other reward this place could offer. The bones
themselves should be wrapped before being carried; they are fragile.
*(Pickup-able with care; quest item for Father Elden.)*

### holy_symbol_pelor
A small disc of tarnished silver hung on a rotted leather thong, fallen from
between the ribs of one of the skeletons. The sun-rayed face of Pelor is
still clearly worked into the metal beneath the tarnish. This was Sera's —
Father Elden's cleric companion, who in his telling went into the dark first
and never came out. Polished, it would still focus a divine spell.
*(Pickup-able; functional holy symbol; significant to Father Elden.)*

### parchment_fragment
A scrap of oiled parchment, brittle but largely intact, folded once and
tucked inside what was once a leather case beside the third set of remains.
Unfolded, it shows a partial map of the cavern system — these chambers,
sketched by a hand working quickly and by uncertain light. Several passages
are marked that do not appear from this side; one, leading off the Pool
Cavern, is annotated in a cramped hand with a single word: *deeper*.
*(Pickup-able; reveals additional map detail; suggests unexplored chambers
beyond the collapsed tunnel.)*

## Notes for DM
- The Echoing Caverns should feel like the campaign's first major dungeon.
- The unrecovered remains are Father Elden's personal quest — restoring them
  to proper burial in Driftmoor will matter to him deeply.
- The Echo Chamber carvings, combined with the obelisk's inscriptions and the
  cliff symbols, spell out more of whatever the obelisk was originally doing.
  Rubbings should be brought to Elyssa; she may recognize fragments.
- The embedded obelisk fragment is the source of the caverns' supernatural
  qualities — destroying or containing it would reduce (not eliminate) the
  shadowbeast threat. Removing the larger Reach obelisk's core is what would
  fully end it.
- Item ids in this file match the `## Items` table in `GEOMETRY.md`. Add new
  items there first (with coordinates), then describe them here.
