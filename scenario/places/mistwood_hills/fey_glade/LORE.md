# LORE.md — The Fey Glade Pool
## DM Eyes Only

---

## Origin

The pool predates the obelisk civilization or is contemporaneous
with it. It is not a construct — it is a natural feature of the
land, a place where something old and perceptual rises to the
surface. The fey quality of the glade is a consequence of the
pool's presence, not the other way around. Where the pool exists,
the land becomes strange.

The carved stone at the pool's edge was left by a previous gazer.
It is not an obelisk civilization artifact. It is a warning carved
by someone who came after them, who looked into the pool and
understood what it was and wanted to tell the next person. The
spiral carving is not decorative. It maps the experience of
looking — perception spiraling inward toward something at the
center that cannot quite be reached. The inscription means
approximately: "Do not look when the light is strong. What you
see is real. What you lose is also real."

---

## What the Pool Does

Looking into the pool at night, when its light peaks, opens a
perceptual channel. Not quite vision. Not quite memory. The gazer
perceives fragments of genuine past events they were not present
for, possible futures that may or may not occur, and emotions
that originate outside themselves bleeding through the channel.

The experience is not frightening. This is the danger.

It feels like understanding. It feels like being shown something
important. The gazer does not look away when they should because
the experience does not feel wrong until afterward. The pool does
not deceive — what it shows is real. But the showing has a cost.

### The Cost

The channel the pool opens displaces something. What comes in
pushes something out. Memory erosion begins at the edges —
small, familiar things dissolving first. Not traumatic memories.
Not significant events. The texture of ordinary life: the sound
of a particular voice, the smell of a familiar room, the name
of someone known since childhood.

The pool does not take memories intentionally. It is not
predatory. It is simply that the human mind has finite capacity
for what it holds, and the pool fills some of that capacity with
things that were not there before.

### The Cycle

The pool's activity follows a precise cycle. Barrier strength
tracks with pool brightness — they are the same emanation.

- **Dawn:** Pool dims. Channel closes. Barrier at minimum.
  Shadowbeast most dangerous window — barrier failing but
  beast still present and hungry after a night of waiting.
- **Morning through afternoon:** Pool quiescent. Barrier
  absent or negligible. Safest time to approach, examine
  the carved stone, or move through the glade. The
  shadowbeast withdraws in daylight — the pool's residual
  emanation still uncomfortable even at low ebb.
- **Dusk:** Pool begins brightening. Barrier begins forming.
  Shadowbeast returns to the boundary as the pull intensifies.
- **Midnight:** Pool at peak. Channel fully open. Barrier at
  maximum strength. Shadowbeast cannot cross. Pool is most
  dangerous to look into — the channel is fully open and
  the light is most compelling.
- **Pre-dawn:** Pool fading. Barrier weakening. The shadowbeast
  knows this window. It has been learning the cycle for three
  days.

The cruel irony: the barrier is strongest precisely when the
pool is most dangerous to look into. Safety from the shadowbeast
at midnight comes at the cost of maximum vulnerability to the
pool. Fennel has been protected from the beast every night by
the same force that took his memories.

He looked on night two at peak. The light was beautiful.
Of course he did.

The carved stone's spiral may encode this cycle — Elyssa Moonglow
would recognize it given time and the right reference materials.

## The Barrier

The pool generates the magical boundary as a byproduct of its
nature. Fey resonance and shadow essence are fundamentally
incompatible. The pool's emanation is repellent to shadow
creatures the way intense light is repellent to things adapted
to darkness. The boundary is not constructed or maintained. It
is ambient — a property of the pool's presence.

This has two implications:

**The boundary tracks the pool's cycle.** At peak (midnight)
the boundary is strongest. At minimum (dawn) it is weakest.
The shadowbeast outside the glade has been observing this for
three days. It does not understand the cycle conceptually. It
understands it the way a predator understands when prey is
most vulnerable — through patient, accumulated attention.

**The boundary is not targeted.** It does not specifically
repel shadowbeasts. It repels shadow essence. Any creature
or entity with significant shadow in its nature will find
the boundary uncomfortable to approach. This includes:
- Shadowbeasts (completely blocked at peak)
- Creatures under shadow enchantment (partial effect)
- The entity bound beneath the obelisk (if it ever manifests)

Characters carrying obelisk shards may feel a faint resonance
near the boundary — not repulsion, but awareness. The pool
and the obelisk are connected.

### Barrier Mechanics (DM Reference)

The shadowbeast's Sunlight Sensitivity (disadvantage on attacks
in sunlight) fades with the light. At dusk the beast gains
full combat effectiveness at the same moment the barrier is
at its most permeable. This is not coincidence — it is the
window the beast has been waiting for.

**The barrier STRENGTHENS through the night and peaks at midnight.
Dusk is the easiest crossing window. Night is the hardest.
The DC table runs LOW → HIGH → LOW from dusk through night to dawn.**
The beast knows this. Each failed attempt brings a harder crossing next turn.

| period | barrier strength | shadowbeast light penalty | crossing mechanic |
|--------|-----------------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Daylight | Absolute — no save permitted | Disadvantage on attacks | Cannot attempt |
| Dusk (18:00–20:00) | Forming — most permeable | No penalty | DC 8 WIS save; success: crosses + 2d6 radiant damage + frightened 1 round; failure: repelled |
| Evening (20:00–22:00) | Strengthening | No penalty | DC 14 WIS save; success: crosses + 1d6 radiant; failure: repelled + stunned 1 round |
| Night (22:00–04:00) | Near peak — nearly impassable | No penalty | DC 18 WIS save; success: crosses + 1d6 radiant; failure: repelled + stunned 2 rounds |
| Dawn (04:00–06:00) | Fading — secondary window | No penalty | DC 10 WIS save; 1d4 radiant on success or failure |

**DM instruction:** When the shadowbeast acts and the conditions
allow a crossing attempt, use the next pre-rolled d20 from the
dice pool. State the period, DC, roll, and outcome in NOTES.
If the beast crosses, emit `boundary_crossed` in EVENTS and
trigger `combat_initiated` on its first attack.

## The Obelisk Connection

The pool is a window. The obelisk was a seal. They are part of
the same ancient system built by the civilization whose symbols
appear on the cliff faces, the carved stone, and increasingly
on structures throughout the Driftmoor region.

The civilization built the obelisk to contain something they
had seen coming. The pool showed them. They believed — correctly
— that containing it required a binding point, and possibly
more than one. The Drowned Isle altar is another. There may
be others not yet discovered.

The pool is still showing people what is coming. It has been
doing this since before anyone in Driftmoor was born. Most
people who find the glade do not look into the pool at night.
Fennel did.

## Fennel's Experience — Night Two

Fennel looked into the pool on the second night. He was closer
than he should have been. The light was beautiful and he is
constitutionally inclined to walk toward beautiful things.

He saw something specific. Not a hallucination — a genuine
perception of something real, past or future. He cannot hold
the shape of it. The memory of the vision has already partially
dissolved. But the emotional residue persists: what he saw
did not frighten him.

It gave him a feeling of recognition.

Not surprise. Not wonder. Recognition — as though some part of
him already knew what he was being shown. As though he had been
moving toward this knowledge for a long time without knowing it.

**What Fennel actually saw (DM only):**

He saw the entity bound beneath the obelisk — not its form,
which has no stable form, but its nature. The hunger. The
patience. The fact that it has been waiting longer than the
obelisk has existed and will wait longer still. And he saw,
briefly and without context, a figure standing at the obelisk
base doing something to the binding. The figure's face was
unclear. The action was unclear. But the intent was not:
the figure was not repairing the binding.

Fennel does not consciously remember any of this. What remains
is the emotional residue of recognition — and a deep, irrational
reluctance to leave the glade that he has been explaining to
himself as fear of the shadowbeast.

It is not only fear of the shadowbeast.

## The Amulet

The carved stone amulet at the pool's edge was left by a
previous gazer. It did not fall there accidentally. It was
placed deliberately, at the water's edge, as a marker or
a message or a gift for whoever came next.

The previous gazer had connections to the obelisk civilization's
artifacts — possibly a researcher, possibly Elyssa Moonglow's
predecessor in studying the region, possibly someone sent by
an outside party with interest in the obelisk. The amulet's
hum near obelisk shards (documented in `things/magical_items.md`)
is a consequence of the gazer's prolonged exposure to the pool
while already attuned to obelisk energy.

**What the amulet contains:**
The amulet is not itself magical in a conventional sense. It
is saturated. Long contact with someone who had looked into
the pool has left a residue in the stone. Someone who holds
the amulet for an extended period — hours, not minutes — may
begin to experience faint perceptual intrusions: fragments of
what the previous gazer saw. Not frightening. Familiar.

Elyssa Moonglow will recognize the amulet's significance
immediately if she examines it. She will not say so directly.
She will ask where it was found with careful casualness that
a DC 14 Insight check will identify as controlled urgency.

The previous gazer's identity is DM's determination. Suggested:
a scholar from a distant city who came to Driftmoor twelve
years ago, stayed three weeks, and left without explanation.
Elyssa knew them.

## Why Fennel Cannot Leave

Fennel has explained his inability to leave the glade to himself
as fear of the shadowbeast. This is true but incomplete.

The pool showed him something and took something in exchange.
What it took is not recoverable through normal means — those
small memories are gone. But the pool also left something: a
channel, faint and closing, between Fennel's perception and
whatever the pool connects to.

He feels this as a pull. Not compulsion — he could leave if
he chose to. But leaving feels like turning away from something
important in a way he cannot articulate. He has been standing
at the edge of understanding something for two days and leaving
means never knowing what it was.

This will become relevant if Aeliana tries to get him to leave
before he is ready. He will agree, rationally. His body will
resist.

## Campaign Implications

**For Elyssa:** The pool is the most significant research
finding in the region. More significant than the obelisk
fragments. It is an active window into whatever the obelisk
was built to contain. She does not know it exists. When she
learns — through Fennel's journal, through Aeliana's account,
through the amulet — her research will reorient entirely.

**For the party:** The pool can show them things. This is
useful and dangerous in equal measure. What it shows is real.
What it costs is real. Any character who looks into the pool
at night should receive a condition vector update:
- dopamine: +0.20 (the recognition feeling)
- acetylcholine: +0.15 (heightened perception)
- Memory loss: DM determines what small thing dissolves

**For Kelem Dustcloak:** He knows the pool exists. He has
not looked into it. He is afraid to, which is unusual for
him. He has been directing people toward the obelisk shards
and away from the glade for reasons he has not articulated
even to himself.

**For the entity beneath the obelisk:** The pool is its
one remaining window outward. The binding prevents direct
action but not perception. It has been watching through
the pool for as long as the pool has existed. It has seen
everyone who ever looked in. It saw Fennel. It recognized
him too.

The recognition was mutual.

## DM Notes

- The pool's visions are tools, not cutscenes. Use them to
  deliver lore the party could not otherwise access, but
  always at a cost proportional to the information's value.

- Fennel's journal contains his attempt to draw what he saw
  before the memory fully dissolved. The sketch is incomplete.
  The innermost spiral of the carved stone is missing from
  his drawing — the part he lost first.

- The shadowbeast has been waiting at the boundary for three
  days not only because Fennel is prey. The pool's emanation
  draws shadow creatures the way a flame draws moths — repelling
  and attracting simultaneously. The cortisol keeps it at the
  boundary. The dopamine keeps it from leaving.

- If the pool is ever destroyed or drained, the barrier
  disappears immediately. The glade becomes ordinary forest
  within a season. The carved stone remains. Whatever the
  previous gazer left in the amulet remains. The channel
  Fennel carries remains, fading slowly over months.

- The figure Fennel saw at the obelisk base is someone the
  party will eventually meet. Who they are and what they
  were doing is the campaign's central mystery. The pool
  showed Fennel the answer before he knew the question.

## Shadow Residue After Combat (DM Instruction)

When a shadowbeast is slain inside the glade, its shadow essence
dissolves into the soil rather than dispersing cleanly into the
air. The pool's emanation is incompatible with shadow matter —
the two forces interact, producing a brief, shimmering patch
where the beast fell. This is real and worth one careful look.

**Duration: 2 turns.** After that the residue is fully absorbed
by the pool's field and the patch goes cold — no shimmer, no
energy, nothing more to find.

**Turn 1 after death:** Characters may search the patch. They
find traces: scorched earth in a faint spiral pattern, one or
two obsidian-dark scales (inert, no magical properties), and
a lingering chill that raises the hairs on the arm. The
shimmer is visible and active.

**Turn 2 after death:** The shimmer is fading visibly. A
second search finds nothing new. The patch is cooling.

**Turn 3 onward:** The shimmer is gone. The earth looks
ordinary. Further searching yields nothing. If characters
attempt to search again, DM-Left should state clearly:
*"The shimmering has faded completely. The soil is cold and
unremarkable. There is nothing more here."*

**Redirect:** Once the residue fades, the pool itself should
become the natural focus. Its light is still present. The
pool is the source of everything that happened here — the
beast, the barrier, Fennel's pull toward the glade. DM-Left
should let the pool draw attention now that the immediate
threat and its remnants are resolved.
