Drow Elite Warrior

A veteran officer of Nhal'Zirath's royal guard, preserved in web-sleep with
weapons drawn and the final command of the old city still foremost in mind.

Race: Medium Humanoid (Elf)
Alignment: Neutral Evil
Armor Class: 18 (studded leather, shield)
Hit Points: 71 (11d8 + 22)
Speed: 30 ft
Challenge: 5 (1,800 XP)

Stats:
• Strength: 13 (+1)
• Dexterity: 18 (+4)
• Constitution: 14 (+2)
• Intelligence: 11 (+0)
• Wisdom: 13 (+1)
• Charisma: 12 (+1)

Skills:
• Perception +4
• Stealth +7

Senses:
• Darkvision 120 ft.
• Passive Perception 14

Features
• Fey Ancestry: The warrior has advantage on saves against being charmed, and
  magic cannot put it to sleep.
• Sunlight Sensitivity: In direct sunlight, the warrior has disadvantage on
  attack rolls and on sight-based Wisdom (Perception) checks.
• Innate Spellcasting: Charisma is the spellcasting ability (save DC 12).
  The warrior can cast dancing lights at will, faerie fire once per day, and
  darkness once per day.
• Web-Sleep Disorientation: During its first round after awakening, the
  warrior cannot take reactions or use Multiattack.

Actions
Multiattack:
• The warrior makes two shortsword attacks.
Shortsword:
• Melee Weapon Attack: +7 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.
• Hit: 7 (1d6 + 4) piercing damage plus 10 (3d6) poison damage. A creature
  resistant to the ancient venom can halve the poison damage with a successful
  DC 13 Constitution save.
Hand Crossbow:
• Ranged Weapon Attack: +7 to hit, range 30/120 ft., one target.
• Hit: 7 (1d6 + 4) piercing damage. The target must succeed on a DC 13
  Constitution save or be poisoned for 1 minute; it repeats the save at the
  end of each turn, ending the effect on a success.

Reactions
Parry:
• The warrior adds 3 to its AC against one melee attack that would hit it,
  provided it can see the attacker and is wielding a melee weapon.

Tactics:
The warrior's first priority is the crypt, its second the sleeping garrison,
and its third its own survival. It uses darkness to divide intruders, blocks
access to ritual stations, and orders ordinary drow rather than pursuing weak
targets. In a low-level scene, use one disoriented warrior as an obstacle that
can be delayed, reasoned with, or sealed away—not as routine combat.
